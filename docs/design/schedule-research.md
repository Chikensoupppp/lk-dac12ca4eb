# How other apps schedule focus time (2026-11-19)

For the "Can't do 4:00?" / calendar schedule feature. Sources at the bottom.

## Three families

**App blockers (Opal, Freedom, Jomo, Brick).** You set a schedule by hand: name, start and end time,
days of the week. None of them read your calendar. The repeating weekly schedule is the paid part:
Opal Pro ("unlimited Rules on a weekly schedule", plus Hard Mode), Freedom Premium ("Recurring
Sessions"). Opal free users get 3 Rules. Opal asks you to "Hold to Commit" before a schedule goes live.
Brick's reviews complain schedules that stay on outside their window.

**Calendar AI (Reclaim; Clockwise shut down March 2026).** You say how much focus time you want a
week; it places the blocks around your meetings and moves them when your calendar changes. Clockwise
used "morning or afternoon" windows. Reclaim only acts after you give permission.

**Planners (Structured, TimeFinder).** They import your calendar into a timeline and show the gaps;
you put tasks in the gaps yourself. Structured starts every day with editable "wake up" and "wind down"
blocks, and makes calendar import a Pro feature. TimeFinder reads the calendar but never writes to it.

## What to take for SwipeLater

1. Ask, don't tell: show the free gaps as choices (planners), not "you're free at 5:30".
2. Don't force one time on every day (Reclaim): if Wednesday 5:30 is busy, offer 6:15 that day.
   "Best free time each day" can be the Plus version of the self-locking schedule.
3. Free = reminders and one-off sessions; Plus = the repeating weekly auto-lock (Opal, Freedom).
4. Ask for the calendar only when someone taps "Can't do 4:00?" (Breakthrough Apps shows its prompt
   on first tap), never during the normal intro.
5. A commit moment before it goes live (Opal "Hold to Commit"); our app already has hold-to-start.
6. Show the next auto-lock on the Focus screen and let it be turned off easily (Brick's complaints).
7. Read only, never write events (TimeFinder).
