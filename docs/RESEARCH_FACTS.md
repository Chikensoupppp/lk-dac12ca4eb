# Real research we can quote (and what we must not say)

Written 2026-11-04. Every line below was checked against a source found in search. Re-check the paper before shipping any wording. Rule: say what the study found, who ran it, and how big it was. Never say Lock In causes a result. We have no study of our own app.

## Safe to use

1. **Minds wander a lot.**
   - Claim: "In a Harvard study of 2,250 adults, minds wandered 46.9% of the time."
   - Source: Killingsworth and Gilbert (2010), "A wandering mind is an unhappy mind", Science. Harvard Gazette covered it.
   - Limits: people were pinged through an iPhone app and answered about themselves. The "wandering causes unhappiness" idea is an inference from correlation, so do not quote the unhappiness part as proven.
   - Link: https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/wandering-mind-not-a-happy-mind/

2. **Even a notification you ignore can hurt.**
   - Claim: "In a 2015 experiment, getting a phone notification hurt performance on an attention task, even when people did not touch the phone."
   - Source: Stothart, Mitchum and Yehnert (2015), Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance, 41(4), 893-897. The effect was about as large as actually using the phone.
   - Limits: a lab task, not a whole workday.

3. **Getting back after an interruption takes time.**
   - Claim: "In workplace research from UC Irvine, it often took over 20 minutes to return to interrupted work."
   - Source: Gloria Mark's fieldwork. The widely quoted number is 23 minutes 15 seconds (interview). The peer-reviewed 2005 CHI paper reports 25 minutes 26 seconds with a very wide spread, and the time includes other work done in between.
   - So say "over 20 minutes", not an exact figure. Old, small study at one workplace.

4. **Part of your attention stays on the last task.**
   - Claim: "Researchers call it attention residue: after you switch tasks, part of your mind stays on the old one."
   - Source: Sophie Leroy (2009), Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 109, 168-181.

## Do not use
- **"Having your phone in the room lowers your brainpower"** (Ward et al., 2017 "Brain Drain"). Several later replications did not find the effect.
- **"People focus for only 47 seconds"**. I could not find a primary source in this search.
- **Any "boosts focus by X%" for Lock In.** We have no data. Making one up would be a lie.
- **"Harvard says..." for anything not from Harvard.** Only item 1 is Harvard.
- **Unverified blog numbers.** One blog cited a 2026 study I could not confirm. Skip it.

## How to show it in the app
- One short fact per welcome slide, small text, with the author and year underneath.
- A "Source" tap opens the full citation. No paragraph on the slide itself.
- Later, replace quotes with the user's own real numbers: their minutes, streak and weekly receipt. That is honest and more personal than any study.
