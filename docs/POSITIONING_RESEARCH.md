# How Lock In can stand out - research notes

Written 2026-11-04. Source quality is mixed: most pages are review roundups and vendor blogs. Treat as signals, not proof. Check prices in the App Store before quoting any.

## What Lock In is meant to be (from PRODUCT_VISION.md)
An iPhone + Windows focus system. One tap starts a synced session on both. The phone applies the strongest allowed distraction controls. Windows prepares a focused workspace. Honest about what Apple allows. Safe recovery. "Launch useful work, not just block distractions."

## Competitors (what they do)
- Opal: strong iPhone blocking with Screen Time. Costly. Users report it can be turned off in iOS Settings mid-session.
- Freedom: blocks across phone and laptop. Its main selling point. Reviews report iPhone blocking that silently fails, a weak Windows client, and billing complaints.
- Forest: gamified timer (grow a tree). No real blocking by itself.
- Cold Turkey: strict Windows blocker. One Sec: adds a pause before opening an app. Others: Jomo, FocusMe, StrictBlock (focus profiles, session journal, weekly report).

## What users complain about
- Blockers that are easy to bypass: toggle a setting, delete the app, one-tap "ignore".
- Silent failure: the app looks active but is not blocking.
- Subscriptions and surprise charges. People look for cheaper, simpler options.
- Novelty fades after a week or two.

## Gap found
No source mentioned a tool that starts the phone and the PC together AND launches the PC workspace (project, docs, music) in one tap. This is an absence in search results, not proof nobody does it.

## Where Lock In can win (ranked)
1. One tap, both devices. Phone and PC start and end together (Milestone 2 pairing). This is the core idea and the hardest to copy.
2. Launch the work, not only block. Windows opens saved work targets, then restores the old setup after (Milestone 3).
3. Honest proof. Show what is really on right now. Warn on the phone if Screen Time access is turned off mid-session. Never show "protected" unless it is. This answers the silent-failure complaint.
4. Warm, friendly design. Most focus apps are dark and strict or cute and gamified. The Cream look is calm and human.
5. Fair price. No subscription trap.

## Nice to have, not a differentiator
Lock screen countdown (Live Activity), streaks and history, app groups, breaks, schedules. Useful, but competitors can match them.

## Honest limits
- Apple lets users remove Screen Time access. Lock In cannot stop that. It can detect it and say so.
- Windows enforcement is not built yet.
- Nothing here was tested with real users.
